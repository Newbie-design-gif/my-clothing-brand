# Project plan, budget & hiring

> Part 10 of 14 of the Vawehall e-commerce scope. Full area: **Project management, budget & working with the developer**.
> 103 work items — 🟢 95 needed at launch · 🟡 6 in phase 2 · ⚪ 2 later.

Priorities: 🟢 **Launch** = the store shouldn't go live without it · 🟡 **Phase 2** = first 3–6 months · ⚪ **Later** = Myntra-scale, only when growth demands it.

## How to structure the engagement (contract type)

- 🟢 **Fixed-scope, milestone-based contract for the initial build** — For a first website with a clear goal (an online store), a fixed price tied to a written scope protects you from the bill growing endlessly; you pay for agreed deliverables, not for hours you cannot verify.
  - *Developer note:* Attach the full requirements list as an annexure to the contract so 'scope' is a document, not a conversation.
- 🟢 **Written scope document signed by both sides before any money changes hands** — Most India small-business website disputes are 'I thought that was included'. A signed list of pages, features, and what is NOT included prevents 90% of fights.
- 🟢 **Change-request process with per-change pricing** — You will think of new ideas mid-project. Agree upfront that anything outside the signed scope is quoted separately in writing (even ₹2,000 items), so additions are a choice, not a surprise invoice.
- 🟢 **Avoid pure hourly billing for the first build** — You cannot judge how long a task 'should' take, so hourly billing puts all risk on you. Hourly is fine later for small tweaks once trust exists.
- 🟢 **Simple one-page agreement covering price, timeline, payment schedule, ownership, confidentiality, and exit** — It does not need to be a lawyer-heavy contract, but it must say in writing: total price, deadline, when you pay what, that everything belongs to you, and what happens if either side walks away.
- 🟢 **Exit clause: you keep all work paid for so far** — If the project goes bad halfway, you must legally be able to take the half-built site, all designs, and all accounts to another developer without a fight.
- 🟡 **Penalty / incentive clause for the deadline (optional but useful)** — A small deduction for every week of unexplained delay (and possibly a small bonus for early delivery) keeps a freelancer focused on your project instead of juggling five others.
- 🟢 **Decide freelancer vs small agency vs platform expert** — A solo freelancer is cheapest but risky if they disappear; a small agency costs 1.5-2x but has backup people; a Shopify-certified expert costs more per hour but finishes faster with fewer mistakes. Pick based on how much hand-holding you need, not just price.
- 🟢 **Ask for GST invoice from the developer/agency** — A proper GST invoice makes the expense legitimate for your business books and lets you claim input tax credit; a developer who refuses to invoice properly is a warning sign.
  - *Developer note:* If paying a freelancer above threshold amounts, founder's accountant should check TDS applicability (Sec 194J professional fees).

## Realistic phase-wise timeline (single-brand store on Shopify/similar)

- 🟢 **Week 1-2: Discovery & planning** — Developer studies your products, competitors, and this requirements list, then confirms scope, platform, and sitemap in writing. Rushing this phase is the number one cause of rework later.
- 🟢 **Week 2-4: Design (homepage, product page, cart/checkout look, mobile-first)** — You should see and approve visual mock-ups of key pages on a phone screen before any building starts, because changing a design later costs 3x more than changing a picture now.
  - *Developer note:* Figma mockups or a customized theme demo; approve mobile view first since 80%+ Indian traffic is mobile.
- 🟢 **Week 4-7: Build & integrations (payments, shipping, WhatsApp, GST settings)** — This is where the store actually gets made: theme setup, Razorpay/UPI/COD, Shiprocket or courier setup, GST-compliant invoicing, WhatsApp notifications.
- 🟢 **Week 6-8 (parallel): Product content upload** — Photographing, writing, and uploading 50-100 products takes far longer than founders expect — often 2-3 full weeks — and it is usually YOUR job unless you pay extra. Start early or it delays launch.
- 🟢 **Week 8-9: Testing & fixes (UAT)** — You and 3-4 friends place real test orders on real phones, try to break things, and the developer fixes what you find before customers ever see it.
- 🟢 **Week 9-10: Soft launch, then public launch** — Open quietly to friends/family and Instagram followers for a few days to catch real-world problems, then announce widely. Total realistic timeline: 8-12 weeks, not the '2 weeks' some vendors promise.
- 🟢 **Add buffer: assume 20-30% schedule slippage** — Delays from payment-gateway approval (Razorpay KYC can take 1-2 weeks), courier account activation, and your own content readiness are normal; plan your marketing launch date with this cushion.
- ⚪ **Custom-built site timeline: 4-6 months minimum** — If you ever choose a fully custom build instead of Shopify/WooCommerce, everything above roughly doubles or triples — a reason NOT to go custom on day 1.

## Realistic budget ranges in INR (2026, India)

- 🟢 **Route A - Shopify (recommended for launch): ₹50,000 - ₹2,00,000 one-time setup** — A freelancer can set up a polished Shopify store for ₹50k-₹1L; a good small agency charges ₹1L-₹2L. This is the fastest, lowest-risk route for a single-brand store.
  - *Developer note:* Plus Shopify subscription roughly ₹2,000-₹7,500/month, premium theme ₹8k-₹30k one-time, apps ₹2k-₹8k/month.
- 🟢 **Route A ongoing costs: ₹4,000 - ₹15,000/month** — Budget for Shopify plan + essential apps + domain renewal; founders often forget the store has a monthly 'rent' even when sales are zero.
- 🟢 **Route B - WooCommerce/WordPress: ₹60,000 - ₹2,50,000 one-time** — Cheaper monthly running cost (hosting ₹8k-₹30k/year) but needs more maintenance and a developer on call; good if you want more control and lower recurring fees.
- 🟢 **Route C - Budget builders (Dukaan, Wix, Instamojo store): ₹10,000 - ₹50,000** — Fine for testing demand with 10-20 products, but you will likely outgrow it in months; count this money as a trial, not an investment.
- ⚪ **Route D - Fully custom build (Next.js/Django etc.): ₹4,00,000 - ₹20,00,000+** — Only makes sense once you have proven sales volume and unique needs no platform serves; at launch it is slower, costlier, and riskier for zero customer-visible benefit.
- 🟢 **Hidden/side costs to budget separately: ₹30,000 - ₹1,00,000 in year 1** — Product photography (₹300-₹800/product), logo/brand assets, payment gateway fees (about 2% per transaction), Shiprocket/courier charges, WhatsApp API tool subscription, GST accountant — none of this is in the developer's quote.
- 🟢 **Content writing / product descriptions: ₹100 - ₹300 per product if outsourced** — If you cannot write 100 descriptions yourself, this line item exists and is always forgotten in budgets.
- 🟢 **Marketing budget is separate from the website budget** — A common failure: spending the entire budget on the site and ₹0 on ads/Instagram, then wondering why there are no orders. Keep at least an equal amount aside for the first 3 months of marketing.

## Milestone-based payment schedule

- 🟢 **Never pay more than 25-30% as advance** — The advance covers the developer's commitment and discovery work; anyone demanding 50-100% upfront is asking you to carry all the risk.
- 🟢 **Suggested split: 25% advance / 25% on design approval / 30% on working store demo / 20% after launch + handover** — Each payment is released only when you can SEE something: approved designs, a store you can click through and place a test order on, and finally a live site with all passwords in your hands.
- 🟢 **Hold the final 15-20% until after handover and a short warranty period** — The last payment is your only leverage for bug fixes and account handover; release it only after the ownership checklist (below) is 100% complete and the site has run 1-2 weeks without major issues.
- 🟢 **Tie every payment to a written, demonstrable deliverable — never to dates alone** — 'Pay 30% on 15th September' rewards the calendar; 'pay 30% when I can place a test COD order on my phone' rewards actual work.
- 🟢 **Pay by bank transfer/UPI against a proper invoice, never cash** — A paper trail protects you in any dispute and keeps your business accounts clean for GST.
- 🟢 **30-60 day free bug-fix warranty written into the contract** — Defects found in the first month after launch (things that were in scope but do not work) should be fixed free — this is standard and must be stated explicitly.

## What a good proposal/quote must itemize

- 🟢 **Platform and exact plan being used (e.g. Shopify Basic, hosting provider for WooCommerce)** — So you know your recurring costs and are not surprised by 'sir, you also need to buy...' later.
- 🟢 **Page-by-page and feature-by-feature list with counts** — 'E-commerce website' means nothing; 'homepage, collection pages, product page template, cart, checkout, 6 policy pages, contact page, size-guide, order tracking page' means everything.
- 🟢 **Named integrations: payment gateway, COD, courier/Shiprocket, WhatsApp notifications, GST invoicing, analytics** — Each integration is real work; if it is not named in the quote, assume it is not included and will cost extra.
- 🟢 **Number of design revisions included (typically 2-3 rounds)** — Unlimited revisions do not exist; knowing the count stops both endless tinkering and surprise charges.
- 🟢 **How many products they will upload, and who prepares images/text** — Product upload is the most commonly disputed grey area; the quote must say '20 products uploaded by us as samples, rest by client' or similar.
- 🟢 **What is explicitly EXCLUDED (logo design, photography, content writing, marketing, paid apps)** — An exclusions list is the sign of an honest vendor; vague quotes hide future charges.
- 🟢 **Timeline with milestone dates and dependencies on you** — The quote should say 'design starts within 3 days of receiving logo and product photos' so delays can be traced to the right side.
- 🟢 **Warranty period, AMC offer, and hourly/per-task rate for future small changes** — Knowing post-launch pricing NOW prevents being held hostage later when you need a banner changed.
- 🟢 **Statement that all accounts, code, and content belong to you** — One line — 'all deliverables, accounts, and intellectual property vest with the client on final payment' — saves enormous pain.
- 🟢 **Compare at least 3 quotes before choosing** — Not to pick the cheapest, but to spot the outliers: a quote 3x cheaper than the others is usually missing scope, not being generous.

## Acceptance criteria & testing checklist (before final payment)

- 🟢 **Place at least 5 end-to-end test orders: UPI, card, netbanking, wallet, and COD** — Every payment method a real customer might use must be tried by you personally, including the order-confirmation email/SMS/WhatsApp that follows.
  - *Developer note:* Use Razorpay/gateway test mode first, then at least 1-2 real ₹1-₹10 live transactions before launch; verify settlement reaches the bank account.
- 🟢 **Test a failed/abandoned payment** — When a UPI payment fails midway (very common in India), the customer must see a clear message and not be charged for a ghost order.
- 🟢 **Full COD flow test including COD verification and courier serviceability** — COD will be 40-60% of your orders; test that COD is offered only for serviceable pincodes and that the order reaches your courier panel correctly.
- 🟢 **Return/exchange flow test: request a return on a test order and process the refund** — Clothing has 15-30% return rates; if the return request and refund process is broken, you will drown in angry WhatsApp messages within a week of launch.
- 🟢 **Mobile testing on at least 3 real phones (one budget Android, one mid Android, one iPhone) on 4G, not office WiFi** — Most of your customers will shop on a ₹10-15k Android phone on mobile data; the site must load in under 3-4 seconds and look right there, not just on the developer's laptop.
- 🟢 **Pincode/shipping-rate check on 5-6 pincodes (metro, small town, Northeast, J&K)** — Shipping charges and delivery estimates must show correctly for far-flung pincodes, or you will lose money on every remote order.
- 🟢 **GST-compliant invoice generated on a test order** — The order invoice must show your GSTIN, tax breakup (CGST/SGST or IGST), and HSN code — check one invoice line by line with your accountant.
- 🟢 **Discount code, out-of-stock, and size-variant tests** — Apply a coupon, try to buy a size that is out of stock, switch sizes/colours on a product — these everyday actions break surprisingly often.
- 🟢 **Order notification tests: do YOU get alerted of a new order by email/WhatsApp/app** — An order you never notice is an order you never ship; confirm the store manager alert works before launch.
- 🟢 **Admin training session: you add a product, change a price, and process an order yourself** — Acceptance is not just 'the site works' — it is 'I can run it'. Insist on a recorded 1-2 hour training video call so you can rewatch it.
- 🟢 **Written UAT sign-off sheet listing every test and its result** — A simple checklist you both sign turns 'testing' from a vibe into a record, and defines exactly what the warranty covers.
- 🟡 **Load sanity check for sale days** — Before your first big sale/campaign, ask the developer to confirm the site can handle a few hundred simultaneous visitors without crashing.

## Launch checklist

- 🟢 **Domain connected with HTTPS (padlock) working** — Customers and payment gateways both require a secure site; a 'Not secure' warning kills trust instantly.
- 🟢 **Payment gateway switched from test mode to live mode and one real order verified** — The single most embarrassing launch failure is customers paying into test mode where no money actually moves.
- 🟢 **All policy pages live: Returns, Shipping, Privacy, Terms, Contact with address & phone** — Razorpay/payment gateways require these pages for approval, and Indian customers check the return policy before trusting a new brand.
- 🟢 **All placeholder text and dummy products removed** — A single 'Lorem ipsum' or ₹0 test product on a live store screams 'unfinished' to a first-time visitor.
- 🟢 **Google Analytics / Meta Pixel installed and firing** — If tracking is not live from day 1, you will never know where your first customers came from, and future ads will be flying blind.
  - *Developer note:* GA4 + Meta Pixel + conversion events (add-to-cart, purchase) verified with test events before launch.
- 🟢 **Google Search Console submitted and store discoverable on Google** — So people searching your brand name actually find your site and not just your Instagram.
- 🟢 **Order alerts, customer emails/WhatsApp, and inventory counts double-checked on launch morning** — A 15-minute final sweep on launch day catches the switch someone forgot to flip.
- 🟢 **Instagram/WhatsApp Business links point to the new site, and the site links back to them** — Your existing followers are your first customers; the loop between social media and store must be seamless.
- 🟢 **Backup taken of the finished site before going live** — A snapshot of the launch-day site means any disaster later can be undone.
- 🟢 **Soft-launch to 20-50 friendly customers before public announcement** — Real strangers-adjacent users will find problems your testing missed, while the audience is still forgiving.
- 🟢 **Developer on standby for launch week** — Agree in writing that the developer is reachable within a few hours during the first 7 days, when issues are most likely and most damaging.

## Post-launch maintenance contract (AMC)

- 🟢 **Sign a simple monthly or annual maintenance agreement, separate from the build contract** — Websites are not 'finished' — apps update, bugs appear, banners need changing. A pre-agreed AMC is far cheaper than emergency per-call pricing.
- 🟢 **Realistic AMC cost: ₹3,000 - ₹10,000/month for a Shopify store; ₹8,000 - ₹25,000/month for WooCommerce/custom** — Shopify needs less care (the platform maintains itself); WooCommerce needs regular updates and security patching, hence higher cost. Annual AMC is often quoted as 15-20% of the build cost.
- 🟢 **AMC must include: bug fixes, platform/plugin updates, and a fixed number of small content changes per month (e.g. 4-6 banner/product tweaks)** — Defining 'small change' quota stops both you over-asking and them over-charging.
- 🟢 **AMC must include response-time promises (e.g. site-down fixed in 4-8 hours, minor bugs in 2-3 days)** — 'We will look into it' is not a service level; during a sale, every hour of downtime is lost revenue.
- 🟡 **AMC must include weekly/monthly backups and a monthly health report** — A one-page monthly note — uptime, backups done, updates applied, speed check — proves you are getting what you pay for.
- 🟢 **AMC must exclude and separately price: new features, redesigns, marketing work** — Clarity on what is 'maintenance' versus 'new work' prevents every future conversation from becoming a negotiation.
- 🟢 **AMC should be cancellable with 30 days notice, no long lock-in** — If service quality drops, you need the freedom to move to another maintainer without penalty.
- 🟡 **Review and renegotiate the AMC after 6 months based on actual usage** — After half a year you will know whether you need more support (growing fast) or less (stable store), and can right-size the fee.

## Ownership & handover checklist (everything in YOUR name)

- 🟢 **Domain registered in your own account (GoDaddy/Namecheap/etc.), paid by your card** — The domain IS your brand online; if the developer registers it in their account, they effectively own your brand name. This is the most common and most painful trap.
- 🟢 **Shopify/hosting account created with your email, you as owner, developer added as 'staff/collaborator'** — Owner can remove staff; staff cannot remove owner. Never accept 'we will make it in our agency account, it is easier'.
- 🟢 **Payment gateway (Razorpay/PayU/Cashfree) account under your PAN/GST, settling to your bank** — Money from customers must flow directly to you; a gateway in anyone else's name means your revenue passes through their hands.
- 🟢 **Courier/Shiprocket, WhatsApp Business, Google Analytics, Search Console, Meta Business accounts — all created under your email with developer as invited user** — Every account created 'for convenience' under the developer's email becomes a hostage negotiation the day you part ways.
- 🟢 **Master password/access sheet handed over and all developer-only passwords changed after handover** — You need a single document listing every account, login email, and where its password lives; then rotate passwords so ex-vendors no longer hold keys.
  - *Developer note:* Recommend the founder keep credentials in a password manager, with 2FA on domain, Shopify/hosting, and payment gateway using founder's own phone number.
- 🟢 **All 2FA/OTP recovery set to YOUR phone number and email** — An account 'in your name' whose OTPs go to the developer's phone is not in your name.
- 🟢 **Source code / theme files copy delivered (for WooCommerce or custom builds: a Git repository you own)** — If the site is custom-coded, the code is the product you paid for; without a copy you cannot ever switch developers.
  - *Developer note:* GitHub organisation owned by founder, developer as collaborator; include database export and deployment instructions in a README.
- 🟢 **All design files, logos, product photos, and raw images handed over in a shared drive you own** — You paid for these assets and will need them for ads, packaging, and future redesigns.
- 🟢 **Paid licenses (theme, plugins, fonts, stock photos) purchased under your accounts with invoices** — If the theme license lives in the agency's bulk account, it dies when your relationship does, and your site breaks at the next update.
- 🟢 **Recorded admin training videos and a simple 'how to run the store' document** — Handover is knowledge, not just passwords: adding products, changing prices, processing orders and refunds should all be documented for you and future staff.
- 🟢 **Verify ownership BEFORE releasing the final payment** — Log out, log back in to every account yourself, from your own devices, before the last rupee moves. Trust, but verify.

## Red flags when hiring a developer

- 🟢 **Demands 50%+ advance or full payment upfront** — Standard practice is 25-30%; large advances remove the developer's incentive to finish.
- 🟢 **Promises a full e-commerce site in 1-2 weeks** — A realistic single-brand store takes 8-12 weeks; impossible timelines mean either a copy-paste template with no testing, or a promise designed to be broken.
- 🟢 **No live store references you can click and no store owner you can phone** — Screenshots can be faked; a 10-minute call with two past clients ('did they deliver on time? do they respond after payment?') is the best due diligence available.
- 🟢 **Insists on registering domain/hosting/accounts under their own name 'to make it easy'** — This is the classic lock-in trap described in the handover section; it is never for your benefit.
- 🟢 **Pushes a fully custom-coded site to a first-time seller** — Custom code maximises the developer's billing and your dependence on them; for a starting brand it is almost never the right answer over Shopify/WooCommerce.
- 🟢 **Vague one-line quotes ('complete website with everything - ₹80,000')** — No itemization means every disagreement later will be settled by whoever shouts louder.
- 🟢 **No written contract, WhatsApp-only 'agreement'** — WhatsApp chats are better than nothing, but a vendor who resists putting scope and price in a signed document is planning flexibility for themselves, not you.
- 🟢 **Cannot clearly answer India-specific questions (COD flow, GST invoicing, Shiprocket, UPI failure handling)** — Plenty of developers have built brochure sites but never a real Indian store; these four topics expose them in one conversation.
- 🟢 **Slow or evasive communication during the SALES process** — Vendors are at their most attentive before you pay; if replies take 4 days now, imagine after the advance.
- 🟢 **Refuses milestone payments, warranties, or ownership clauses** — Every professional accepts these; refusal signals either inexperience or intent.
- 🟢 **Quotes dramatically below market (e.g. full store for ₹15,000)** — You will get a pirated theme, no testing, and an unreachable phone number; cheap becomes expensive.
- 🟢 **No mention of testing, mobile checks, or post-launch support in their pitch** — Building is half the job; a vendor who never mentions the other half does not do it.

## Communication cadence & working rhythm

- 🟢 **One fixed weekly 30-minute review call (video, with screen share)** — A standing weekly call keeps the project moving and surfaces delays within days instead of at the deadline; seeing the actual work-in-progress beats status reports.
- 🟢 **One shared WhatsApp group for the project, but decisions confirmed in writing (email or a shared doc)** — WhatsApp is fine for quick questions, but scope and approval decisions must live somewhere searchable — 'as discussed on call, we approved X' emails take 2 minutes and end disputes before they start.
- 🟢 **Single point of contact on each side** — If your cousin, your partner, and you all give the developer instructions, contradictions become the developer's excuse; nominate one decision-maker (you).
- 🟢 **Shared live checklist/tracker (Google Sheet or Trello) of all tasks with status** — A simple shared list — To do / In progress / Done / Blocked-on-founder — makes progress visible daily without you having to ask.
- 🟢 **Agreed response-time norms (e.g. replies within 1 business day both ways)** — Cadence cuts both ways: developers stall waiting for founder approvals as often as founders wait for developers.
- 🟢 **Milestone demo meetings where you approve or reject in writing within 3 days** — Approval deadlines on YOUR side keep you from becoming the bottleneck and give the timeline honest accountability.
- 🟡 **Keep a running 'decisions log' — one line per decision with date** — Six weeks in, nobody remembers why COD limit was set to ₹3,000; a decisions log is your project's memory.
- 🟡 **Monthly review meeting after launch (during AMC)** — A short monthly call covering sales, site issues, and next improvements turns maintenance from silence-until-crisis into steady progress.

## Questions to ask your developer

- Which platform do you recommend for a single-brand clothing store in India and why — Shopify or WooCommerce — and what will my total monthly running cost be, including apps/plugins?
- Can you show me 2-3 live Indian e-commerce stores you built, and give me phone numbers of those owners as references?
- Have you integrated Razorpay/UPI, COD with pincode-based serviceability, and Shiprocket before? Walk me through how a failed UPI payment is handled on your builds.
- How will GST-compliant invoices (GSTIN, CGST/SGST/IGST breakup, HSN codes) be generated on each order?
- Exactly how many products will you upload, and in what format do you need my photos, prices, and size charts?
- How many design revision rounds are included, and what does an extra round cost?
- What is your milestone plan, and can we tie each payment to a demonstrable deliverable rather than a date?
- What is explicitly NOT included in this quote?
- Will the domain, Shopify/hosting, payment gateway, and all other accounts be created in MY name with you as invited staff? Please confirm in writing.
- What free bug-fix warranty period do you give after launch, and what does your AMC include, cost, and exclude?
- What are your response-time commitments if the site goes down during a sale?
- How will we test before launch — can you share your testing checklist covering test orders, all payment methods, COD, returns, and real budget-Android phones on 4G?
- Who is my single point of contact, what is our weekly meeting slot, and how do you handle it if you are sick or travelling mid-project?
- If we part ways mid-project or after launch, what exactly do I walk away with, and how long does handover take?
- What is your hourly/per-task rate for small changes after launch (banner swaps, new page, festival sale setup)?
- Will you provide a GST invoice for your fees?
- How will the site handle a traffic spike during a festival sale, and is there any extra cost for that?
- What happens to my site if a Shopify app or WordPress plugin you chose gets discontinued or starts charging more?

## What you (the owner) must provide

- [ ] Final decision on platform route and total budget ceiling (build + monthly running + marketing kept separate)
- [ ] Business documents for accounts: GSTIN, PAN, current account details, cancelled cheque — needed for payment gateway and courier KYC (do the Razorpay/gateway KYC yourself; it can take 1-2 weeks, start early)
- [ ] Domain name choice (buy it yourself, in your own account, before the project starts)
- [ ] Logo and brand assets (colours, fonts if any); commission these separately if they do not exist
- [ ] Product catalogue in a spreadsheet: names, descriptions, prices, sizes, colours, stock counts, HSN codes
- [ ] Product photos (flat-lay or model shots) — budget for a photoshoot; this is usually the single biggest launch delay
- [ ] Size charts with actual garment measurements for each product type
- [ ] Business policies decided in writing: return/exchange window and conditions, refund method, shipping charges, COD limits, delivery timelines
- [ ] Registered business address and customer-support phone/WhatsApp number to publish on the site
- [ ] Your own email address (not the developer's) used to create every account, with 2FA on your own phone
- [ ] A decision-maker (you) available for weekly calls and able to approve milestones within 3 days
- [ ] 3-4 friends/family with different phones (budget Android + iPhone) to help with pre-launch test orders
- [ ] An accountant to verify the GST invoice format on test orders
- [ ] Signed contract and UAT sign-off sheet (keep copies of everything: quote, contract, invoices, decisions log)
- [ ] First month's marketing plan and budget so the store is not launched into silence
