## Payments & taxes for USA customers

### Choosing a payment gateway (this decision comes before everything else)

- 🟢 **Accept that the India gateway plan changes completely** — Razorpay/Cashfree in domestic mode, UPI, netbanking and COD were built for Indian buyers paying in rupees. US customers pay in dollars with US cards, PayPal and Apple/Google Pay, and your money must legally come into India as an export payment. The whole 'Payment gateway & online payment methods' section of the old scope is replaced by this one.
  - *Developer note:* Do not reuse the domestic Razorpay integration as-is. Checkout presents USD only; INR never appears to the customer.
- 🟢 **PayPal Business (India) as the launch anchor** — PayPal accepts Indian businesses, is trusted by US shoppers (many will only buy from an unknown foreign store if PayPal is offered), charges in USD, converts and deposits INR to your Indian bank automatically, and issues the FIRC paperwork you need as export proof. It is the fastest legally-clean way for an Indian entity to take US money.
  - *Developer note:* PayPal Checkout (Smart Buttons) — gives PayPal balance, US cards via PayPal, and often Pay Later ('Pay in 4') to US buyers automatically. Expect roughly 4.4% + fixed fee cross-border plus a currency-conversion spread; surface real net-of-fee numbers to the owner.
- 🟢 **A direct card gateway alongside PayPal (Razorpay/Cashfree/PayU international mode)** — Some US shoppers will not use PayPal and just want to type a card. Your existing Indian gateways can accept international cards, but it is a separate approval: you apply for 'international payments' activation, they review your website and policies, and settlement still lands in INR in your Indian account.
  - *Developer note:* Razorpay international requires activation and website compliance (visible pricing in USD, refund/shipping/privacy/contact pages, business details in footer). Confirm USD presentment (customer charged in USD, not INR) — otherwise US banks may add foreign-transaction fees and buyers get spooked. Check Amex/Discover support explicitly.
- 🟢 **Stripe India: check eligibility, do not assume it** — Stripe is the best gateway for US-style checkout (cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, great fraud tools), but Stripe India has been closed/invite-only for new Indian businesses for a while. If you can get an account, strongly prefer it; if not, do not stall the launch waiting.
  - *Developer note:* Verify current Stripe India onboarding status at project start. If available: Stripe Checkout/Payment Element gives Apple Pay + Google Pay + Link with near-zero extra work, USD presentment, INR settlement, and Radar fraud screening.
- 🟢 **Shopify Payments is NOT available to an India-registered business** — If the store is built on Shopify, know upfront that Shopify's own gateway (and therefore Shop Pay and its native Apple Pay) requires a business entity in a supported country — India is not one. You will run Shopify with PayPal plus a third-party gateway, and Shopify charges an extra transaction fee (0.5–2%) on top when you do.
  - *Developer note:* Factor the extra Shopify third-party-gateway fee into pricing. If not on Shopify this item is moot, but the same logic applies: no US-entity-only gateway will onboard the Indian company.
- 🟢 **Merchant-of-record shortcuts mostly do not apply to clothing** — Paddle and 2Checkout's '2Monetize' handle taxes for you but only for digital products — they will not touch physical apparel. 2Checkout's standard '2Sell' plan does accept Indian sellers for physical goods, but it is just another gateway (higher fees, ~3.5%+), not a magic tax solution. Do not let anyone sell you 'Paddle handles everything' — it cannot for T-shirts.
  - *Developer note:* 2Checkout/Verifone 2Sell is a legitimate backup if Razorpay international approval fails. Evaluate only as a card processor, not as merchant of record.
- 🟢 **Cross-border fee reality baked into pricing** — In India you paid roughly 2% per transaction. For US sales expect 3–5% gateway fees PLUS 1–4% currency conversion, so 5–8% of every order disappears before it reaches your bank. Your USD prices must be set knowing this, or your margins quietly vanish.
  - *Developer note:* Build a simple per-order economics sheet: USD price → gateway fee → FX spread → INR received. Show 'INR realized' per order in admin (phase 2 report is fine, the pricing math is launch).
- ⚪ **US LLC route (Stripe Atlas / Delaware LLC) for full access** — Forming a US company unlocks Stripe US, Shopify Payments, Shop Pay, direct Klarna/Afterpay and cheaper domestic processing — but it brings US federal/state tax filings, a US bank account, registered-agent costs, and real accounting complexity. Worth it only once US revenue is meaningful, not at launch.
  - *Developer note:* If/when done, keep the checkout gateway-agnostic so swapping PayPal+Razorpay for Stripe US is a config change, not a rebuild.

### Payment methods US shoppers actually expect

- 🟢 **Credit and debit cards, including Amex and Discover** — Cards are the default US payment method the way UPI is in India. US shoppers also use American Express and Discover far more than Indians do — a store that rejects Amex looks broken to a chunk of buyers.
  - *Developer note:* Confirm the chosen gateway's international activation includes Amex and Discover, not just Visa/Mastercard. Card fields must support US formats: ZIP code in billing address (needed for AVS), no OTP step expected.
- 🟢 **PayPal button, prominent** — For a brand nobody in the US has heard of, PayPal is your trust badge: buyers know PayPal will side with them if something goes wrong, so they will risk a first order. Hiding it costs sales.
  - *Developer note:* Show PayPal as an express option at top of checkout, not buried under card fields.
- 🟢 **Apple Pay and Google Pay** — Most of your US traffic will be mobile, and one-tap wallet payment is the single biggest mobile conversion booster in the US market. The catch: availability depends entirely on which gateway you get — Stripe gives both easily; Razorpay international generally does not.
  - *Developer note:* This is a gateway-selection criterion, not an afterthought. If launch stack is PayPal+Razorpay without native Apple Pay, note the gap explicitly and revisit; PayPal's wallet covers some of it. Requires HTTPS and domain verification (Apple).
- 🟡 **Buy Now Pay Later: Klarna / Afterpay / Affirm** — BNPL is huge for US apparel and raises average order value, but Klarna/Afterpay/Affirm onboard merchants with US (or supported-country) entities — an Indian company generally cannot integrate them directly. The free workaround at launch: US buyers who choose PayPal automatically see 'Pay in 4' with zero work or risk on your side.
  - *Developer note:* At launch, enable PayPal Pay Later messaging on product pages (free conversion lift). Direct Klarna/Afterpay integration becomes possible if a US entity is formed (see 'later' item) — park until then.
- 🟢 **Checkout with no OTP/3D-Secure step** — Indian card payments always pause for an OTP; US payments simply do not — the card is charged instantly. Good news: less friction. Bad news: without bank OTPs, fraud liability sits on YOU, which is why the fraud section below matters much more than it did in India.
  - *Developer note:* Remove any UI/logic that waits for OTP/redirect on cards. Keep webhook-as-source-of-truth, idempotency keys, and double-charge protection from the India scope — those carry over unchanged.
- 🟡 **Express checkout row (wallets/PayPal at the top of checkout)** — US stores show Apple Pay/Google Pay/PayPal buttons before the address form — returning wallet users skip typing everything and convert in seconds.
  - *Developer note:* Wallets return verified shipping/billing addresses; pre-fill the form from wallet data.

### India-only features to remove or replace (saves money — cut these from the old scope)

- 🟢 **Remove Cash on Delivery entirely** — COD does not exist in mainstream US e-commerce and no India-to-US courier will collect cash. Everything COD in the old scope goes away: COD option, COD fees, COD pincode checks, COD OTP verification, COD refund payout links, RTO risk rules. This is a large chunk of scope you no longer pay to build.
  - *Developer note:* Delete COD from checkout, admin settings, and order states. 'Convert-to-prepaid nudge' and 'COD refund via UPI payout' items are obsolete.
- 🟢 **Remove UPI, netbanking, Indian wallets and Indian EMI** — US customers have never heard of UPI, GPay-India, Paytm or netbanking. Offering them confuses buyers and signals 'this store is not for me'.
  - *Developer note:* Also remove UPI 'payment pending 5–10 min' handling; US card/wallet payments resolve in seconds. Keep a generic pending state for PayPal eChecks, which can take 3–5 days.
- 🟢 **Replace phone-OTP login with email-first guest checkout** — The old scope's 'login via mobile OTP, guest checkout with phone number' is an Indian pattern. US shoppers expect: guest checkout with an email address, optional password account, and no SMS OTPs (sending OTP SMS to US numbers is also expensive and looks odd).
  - *Developer note:* Email becomes the customer key instead of phone. WhatsApp order-update flows also don't apply to US buyers — email (and optional SMS shipping updates) replace them; that detail belongs to the communications area but affects checkout consent checkboxes here.
- 🟢 **Prices shown WITHOUT tax, in USD (opposite of MRP)** — India shows tax-inclusive MRP; the US convention is the sticker price excludes sales tax, and tax (if any) appears as a separate line at checkout. Showing 'tax included' pricing to Americans reads as strange and makes your prices look higher than competitors'.
  - *Developer note:* Reverse the India-scope rule 'all prices tax-inclusive'. Price breakup: items, discount, shipping, tax line (will be $0 at launch — see sales-tax section), total. USD formatting: $49.00, cents always shown or always .00 consistently.
- 🟢 **Billing address separate from shipping address** — US checkouts always ask 'billing address same as shipping?' because the card's billing ZIP is checked against the bank's records to block fraud. Indian checkouts never needed this; yours now does.
  - *Developer note:* Billing address with ZIP feeds AVS. Default 'same as shipping' checked. US address format: name, street 1/2, city, state (2-letter dropdown), 5-digit ZIP (+4 optional), phone optional.
- 🟢 **GST invoice machinery is replaced, not deleted** — You still need invoices — but the customer-facing invoice is now a simple USD receipt with no GST lines, while a separate export invoice exists for Indian compliance (next section). The CGST/SGST/IGST split logic, GST rate slabs and buyer-GSTIN field are all irrelevant for US sales.
  - *Developer note:* Customer receipt: USD amounts, no tax breakup (until sales tax applies), brand + support contact. Keep sequential numbering. Drop state-based CGST/SGST logic entirely.

### Getting paid: USD to INR settlement and export paperwork

- 🟢 **Customer pays USD, you receive INR — know the exchange path** — The customer must see and be charged pure USD end-to-end. The gateway then converts to INR and deposits to your Indian account, taking a conversion margin. You should know exactly what one $50 order becomes in rupees after all cuts, because that number — not the sticker price — is your real revenue.
  - *Developer note:* Confirm USD presentment + INR settlement with each gateway. Show settlement FX rate per payout in the reconciliation view. PayPal alternative: hold USD and convert manually is NOT available to Indian accounts — auto-withdrawal to INR is forced; plan around it.
- 🟢 **FIRC / FIRA for every settlement — your proof of export money** — A Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate is the bank document proving dollars legally entered India for your exports. Your CA needs it for GST refunds, e-BRC and any future scrutiny. PayPal and Razorpay issue these (PayPal via its partner bank, monthly); they must be collected and stored, not lost in an inbox.
  - *Developer note:* Automate or calendar the FIRC/FIRA download per settlement cycle; store against payout records. Verify the remittance carries the correct RBI purpose code for export of goods (e.g. P0102 family) — usually gateway-handled, but confirm once.
- 🟢 **IEC — Import Export Code — before the first shipment** — Selling goods abroad commercially requires an IEC from DGFT. It is a simple, cheap online registration in the business's name, but couriers and banks will ask for it — no IEC, no clean exports.
  - *Developer note:* Owner action (see checklist). Site/admin should store IEC to print on export invoices and shipping docs.
- 🟢 **GST on exports: zero-rated, file an LUT, never charge the customer GST** — Exports are 'zero-rated' under GST — the US customer pays no Indian tax. To ship without first paying IGST out of pocket, your business files a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) on the GST portal once per financial year (a CA does this in an hour). Without an LUT you'd pay IGST on each export and claim refunds later — pointless pain.
  - *Developer note:* Export invoices must carry the wording 'Supply meant for export under LUT without payment of IGST'. GST filings still report these sales as zero-rated exports; the sales export report should mark them accordingly.
- 🟢 **Export invoice generated per order (the compliance twin of the customer receipt)** — Each order needs an export invoice for Indian records: USD value (with INR conversion), your IEC and GSTIN, HSN codes, buyer's US address, LUT declaration, sequential numbering. The customer never sees this one — it feeds customs paperwork, your CA, and refund claims.
  - *Developer note:* Auto-generate alongside the customer receipt. Keep the India scope's sequential financial-year numbering and credit-note series — both still required, now on export invoices.
- 🟢 **Ship under CSB-V (commercial courier export), not CSB-IV** — Courier exports file either CSB-IV (gifts/samples, no benefits) or CSB-V (commercial exports). Only CSB-V shipments can later support GST refunds and export incentives like RoDTEP/duty drawback. Many small sellers ship 'as gifts' and permanently lose those benefits — decide correctly from order one, because it cannot be fixed retroactively.
  - *Developer note:* This is configured with the courier/aggregator (belongs to shipping area operationally) but the invoice/HSN data your system produces must match CSB-V requirements — coordinate the data contract.
- 🟡 **e-BRC reconciliation (SOFTEX does NOT apply to you)** — The e-BRC (electronic Bank Realisation Certificate) is the bank's confirmation on the DGFT portal that payment for a specific export shipment actually arrived — the government's way of matching goods out to money in. Note: 'SOFTEX' forms you may hear about are only for software/services exporters; physical clothing uses shipping bills + e-BRC instead.
  - *Developer note:* Provide an export report matching orders ↔ shipments ↔ payouts so the CA/bank can generate e-BRCs without spreadsheet archaeology. Monthly cadence is fine.
- 🟡 **USD/INR reconciliation view** — The India scope's 'settlement reconciliation' survives but gets a currency dimension: every payout now involves an exchange rate, and you need to see USD ordered vs INR received per payout to catch missing money and understand FX cost.
  - *Developer note:* Extend the reconciliation export: order USD, gateway fee, FX rate, INR credited, payout reference; monthly CSV for the accountant.

### US sales tax — the honest picture for a foreign seller

- 🟢 **At launch you almost certainly collect NO US sales tax — and that's legal** — US sales tax is owed state by state, and only where you have 'nexus'. With no US office, staff or warehouse, you'd only get 'economic nexus' by crossing thresholds like $100,000 of sales or 200 transactions into a single state in a year. A new brand is far below every state's line, so checkout simply doesn't charge sales tax. This is normal — don't let anyone sell you an expensive tax setup on day one.
  - *Developer note:* Tax line shows $0.00 or is hidden until enabled. Do NOT charge GST or any Indian tax to US customers either. Keep the tax calculation behind a pluggable interface so enabling a state later is configuration, not surgery.
- 🟢 **Track sales per US state from day one** — You can only know you're approaching a state's threshold if you count revenue and order numbers per state. A simple report reviewed monthly is your early-warning system — crossing a threshold unknowingly and not registering is where foreign sellers get into actual trouble.
  - *Developer note:* Report: per state, rolling 12-month revenue and transaction count vs that state's threshold. Ship-to state is the key. This is cheap to build now, painful to reconstruct later.
- ⚪ **When a state threshold is crossed: register, collect, file — with software** — Once you hit nexus in a state, you register with that state, start collecting its tax at checkout, and file returns. Doing this by hand across states is a nightmare; tools like Shopify Tax, TaxJar, Avalara or Numeral calculate rates and (some) file returns. A foreign business can register but may need a US tax ID (EIN) — get a US-familiar tax adviser when this day approaches.
  - *Developer note:* Design checkout to accept a per-state 'collect tax' flag + rate source from a tax API. Apparel has quirks (some states exempt clothing under price caps, e.g. NY/NJ/PA rules) — reason to use an engine, never hand-rolled rates.
- 🟡 **A US warehouse changes everything overnight** — The moment you stock inventory in a US fulfillment center (3PL, Amazon FBA), you have PHYSICAL nexus in that state — tax registration and collection obligations start immediately regardless of revenue. Any future 'ship faster from a US warehouse' decision must loop back to this section first.
  - *Developer note:* Flag this dependency in the fulfillment/shipping addendum. FBA can create nexus in multiple states as Amazon moves stock around.
- 🟡 **Marketplace sales are taxed for you — but still count** — If you also sell on Amazon or Etsy, those marketplaces collect and remit US sales tax on your behalf (marketplace facilitator laws) — nothing for you to do there. But in many states those sales still count toward YOUR thresholds for your own website, so the per-state report should include them.
  - *Developer note:* If marketplace channels launch, feed their per-state sales into the nexus-tracking report.
- 🟢 **Customer-side import duties: the de minimis rules changed** — Separately from sales tax: the US ended its $800 duty-free 'de minimis' allowance for inbound parcels in 2025, so shipments from India can be charged import duties. If the customer gets a surprise duty bill on the doorstep, you get refused parcels and one-star reviews. You must decide: DDP (you prepay duties and bake them into price/checkout) or DDU (customer pays on delivery — risky). This is priced/shown at checkout, so it lands partly in this area even though couriers execute it.
  - *Developer note:* Coordinate with the shipping addendum. If DDP: courier/platform provides landed-cost calculation; show 'duties included' messaging at checkout — it's a conversion advantage. Verify current duty treatment for apparel HS codes from India with the courier at build time, as rules have been shifting.

### Chargebacks & fraud — your biggest new risk

- 🟢 **Understand the chargeback game before the first order** — A US cardholder can dispute a charge with their bank in two clicks; the money is yanked back, you pay a $15–25 fee even if you win, and if disputes exceed roughly 1% of transactions the gateway can terminate your account. In India the OTP protected you; in the US, protection is evidence and prevention. This risk is why every item below exists.
- 🟢 **AVS and CVV checks on every card payment** — Address Verification (billing ZIP matches the bank's records) plus CVV are the standard US anti-fraud checks. They're cheap, built into gateways, and filter the bulk of stolen-card attempts.
  - *Developer note:* Configure gateway rules: decline on AVS ZIP mismatch and CVV fail (or route to manual review); log AVS/CVV results on the order for dispute evidence.
- 🟢 **Tracked delivery with confirmation on every order — your dispute armor** — The most common dispute is 'item not received'. A tracking number showing delivered-to-address wins it; no tracking means automatic loss. For high-value orders, signature on delivery is worth the couple of dollars.
  - *Developer note:* Store tracking + delivery-scan data on the order; make it one click to export as dispute evidence. Signature threshold configurable (e.g. orders over $150).
- 🟢 **Clear billing descriptor with your brand name** — If the card statement says 'RAZORPAY*8842XY' instead of your brand, honest customers won't recognize the charge and dispute it. 'I don't recognize this' chargebacks are pure self-inflicted loss.
  - *Developer note:* Set the statement descriptor to the brand name (+ support phone/URL where the gateway allows). Verify what actually prints for each gateway — international-mode descriptors are often the aggregator's name; this can be a gateway-selection factor.
- 🟢 **Honest India-to-US delivery promises everywhere** — Shipping from India takes 7–14+ days. If checkout implies Amazon-speed and reality is two weeks, buyers assume scam and dispute before the parcel lands. Stating the real timeline at product page, checkout and confirmation email prevents the single most common cross-border chargeback.
  - *Developer note:* Show shipping window at checkout and in the confirmation email; send tracking as soon as the label exists — an in-transit email dramatically cuts 'where is my order' disputes.
- 🟢 **PayPal Seller Protection hygiene** — PayPal will side with you in disputes only if you follow its rules: ship to exactly the address PayPal provides, with tracking, and keep proof. Break the rules and protection is void.
  - *Developer note:* Never let ops override the PayPal-supplied ship-to address; store the PayPal transaction ID against the shipment.
- 🟢 **Cross-border fraud review rules (upgrade of the India manual-review queue)** — The old scope's manual-review flag survives, but the triggers change: billing country different from shipping country, very large first orders, several failed card attempts then success, freight-forwarder addresses, disposable emails. A hold-and-verify queue for these catches most fraud at your scale for free.
  - *Developer note:* Keep the admin hold/approve/cancel queue from the India scope; swap the trigger list for cross-border signals. Maintain email/address/card-fingerprint blocklist (replaces phone/pincode blocklist).
- 🟡 **Selective 3D-Secure on risky orders** — In the US, 3DS is optional — but when it runs and passes, fraud liability shifts to the bank instead of you. Firing it only on suspicious orders gets protection without adding friction to everyone.
  - *Developer note:* Gateway-dependent (Stripe Radar rules do this natively). Skip if the launch gateway can't; revisit with gateway changes.
- 🟡 **Dispute response workflow with deadlines** — Disputes have hard reply deadlines (often 7–20 days). You need an alert when one opens, a template with the evidence to attach (tracking, AVS result, customer emails, policy screenshots), and a log of outcomes so you learn what's winning.
  - *Developer note:* At minimum: email alert on dispute webhook + an admin page linking the order's evidence bundle. Paid tools (Signifyd, NoFraud, Chargeflow) are 'later' — overkill until volume.

### Refunds in USD

- 🟢 **Refund the exact USD amount to the original payment method** — The customer must get back precisely the dollars they paid — $49.00 charged means $49.00 refunded — no matter what the rupee did in between. The exchange-rate difference (and usually the lost gateway fee) is your cost of doing returns; it belongs in your return-policy math, not on the customer.
  - *Developer note:* One-click refund from admin via gateway/PayPal API in USD. Most processors do not return the original processing fee on refund and FX is crossed twice — surface true refund cost per order in reporting.
- 🟢 **Refund timeline messaging: 5–10 business days** — US card refunds take 5–10 business days to appear on statements. Saying so at refund time (and on the returns page) pre-answers the 'where is my refund' emails — same lesson as the India scope, different numbers.
  - *Developer note:* Include timeline + gateway reference ID in the refund email; refund status visible in the customer's order history.
- 🟢 **Delete the COD-refund machinery** — The India scope's UPI/bank payout links for COD refunds are obsolete — every US order has an original payment method to refund to. One less system to build and pay for.
- 🟢 **A written, posted refund policy is legally load-bearing in the US** — Several US states (California most famously) require your refund policy to be clearly posted — and if it isn't, the customer can be entitled to a full refund by default. Card networks and gateway underwriting also demand a visible policy. Decide the window, who pays return shipping (returns to India are expensive — many small brands do 'refund without return' on low-value items), and restocking rules, then publish them.
  - *Developer note:* Policy page linked from checkout footer and confirmation email (gateways check for this during international-payments approval). The 'refund without physical return' decision materially changes the returns flow — coordinate with the returns/shipping area.
- 🟡 **Partial refunds and store credit** — Partial refunds cover one-item-of-three returns and 'keep it, here's 30% back' defect resolutions — often cheaper than a return shipment from the US. Store credit as a first offer saves the gateway fee and FX loss, but must always be the customer's choice, never forced, or it triggers disputes.
  - *Developer note:* Partial refund API support varies — verify per gateway. Store credit is the same mini-system flagged 'later' in the India scope; a simple discount-code-as-credit works as an interim.
- 🟡 **Credit notes against export invoices** — Every refund still needs a matching credit note in your Indian books — now against export invoices — so the export figures your CA reports reconcile with money actually kept.
  - *Developer note:* Same sequential credit-note series requirement as the India scope, applied to export invoices; include in the accountant's monthly export.

## Shipping & operations from India to USA customers

### The headline change: what is different vs selling inside India

- 🟢 **Every US-bound parcel now pays US import duty — the old $800 duty-free rule is GONE** — Until Aug 2025, parcels under $800 entered the US duty-free (the 'de minimis' rule). Executive Order 14324 suspended this for ALL countries effective Aug 29, 2025, and US Customs made the suspension indefinite by regulation on June 24, 2026 (a permanent statutory repeal follows July 1, 2027). Verified via the Federal Register (federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670) and trade-compliance sources (ghy.com, globalior.com). Practical meaning: every single customer order you ship from India — even a $30 t-shirt — goes through customs and owes duty. This reshapes your pricing, margins, and fulfillment choice. Do NOT rely on older blog posts or advice that say 'under $800 ships duty-free' — that is outdated.
  - *Developer note:* All landed-cost math, checkout duty messaging, and carrier integration must assume duties on 100% of orders. No de-minimis logic anywhere.
- 🟢 **How much duty? The India–US tariff rate is VOLATILE — confirm the week you launch** — The picture changed three times in 12 months: 50% punitive tariffs on Indian goods in 2025; a US–India trade deal announced Feb 2, 2026 reportedly setting an effective ~18% reciprocal rate including textiles/apparel (whitehouse.gov joint statement; india-briefing.com; cleartax.in); and reports of tariffs being restructured after a Supreme Court ruling with a ~15% 'temporary import surcharge' from Feb 24, 2026. On TOP of any India-specific rate sits the normal US apparel duty by garment type (roughly 16.5% for cotton tees up to ~32% for synthetics). I cannot give you one reliable number — treat 'total duty = 25–50% of declared value' as the planning range and get the exact current rate IN WRITING from DHL/FedEx or a US customs broker for your exact products before you set retail prices. Re-check quarterly; this is a moving target.
  - *Developer note:* Do not hard-code duty rates. If duties are estimated at checkout, the rate must be a config value or come live from a duty API (Zonos, carrier APIs).
- 🟢 **Shipping cost per order is 5–15x what domestic India shipping costs** — A domestic Indian courier delivers a 0.5 kg parcel for Rs 50–100. India-to-US express costs $15–60 per parcel plus duties. This single fact drives your minimum viable order value: selling a single $25 tee shipped individually from India is usually unprofitable. Expect to push average order value up (bundles, free-shipping thresholds around $75+) or move inventory to the US.

### Fulfillment model — the biggest decision (three options)

- 🟢 **Option A: Ship each order from India (recommended for launch)** — You hold stock in India and courier each order to the customer. Lowest upfront cost, no inventory risk abroad, works from day one with small volumes. Carriers/routes: (1) India Post — resumed US service Oct 15, 2025 after a 2-month halt, now collects US duties upfront (DDP) with no extra facilitation fee; cheapest ($10–20 per 0.5 kg) but 2–4 weeks and patchy tracking — OK for testing, not for a brand experience. Note: postal-route duty was reported as a flat 50% of declared value under the 2025 rules (thefederal.com, newsonair.gov.in) — confirm the current postal duty structure before using it. (2) Express couriers DHL/FedEx direct: 4–7 business days, excellent tracking, $30–60 per 0.5 kg at retail rates. (3) Aggregators — Shiprocket X, ShipGlobal, and similar: discounted express/economy rates ($15–30 per 0.5 kg), 5–10 business days, DDP support, auto-generated customs paperwork, one dashboard. Best starting point for a small brand. (4) Aramex/economy express: $12–25, 8–12 days, mid-tier tracking.
  - *Developer note:* Integrate ONE aggregator API first (Shiprocket X or ShipGlobal). Orders push automatically, labels + commercial invoices generate from order data, tracking numbers sync back.
- 🟡 **Option B: US 3PL warehouse (ShipBob, ShipMonk, Flexport, etc.) — the scale-up move** — You bulk-ship inventory to a US warehouse once (air or sea freight), pay duty once on the WHOLESALE value (much lower than retail declared values), then each customer order ships domestically in the US: 2–5 day delivery, $4–10 per order, easy returns. This is what makes you feel like a US brand. Costs: freight ($300–800 per air-shipped carton batch; sea cheaper but 4–6 weeks), duty on the bulk entry, storage ($20–40+ per pallet/month), pick-pack fees ($2.50–5 per order), and a one-time setup as a Foreign Importer of Record (needs a US customs bond ~$300–600/yr and a customs broker; you do NOT need a US company, though many founders form an LLC anyway for banking/returns). Trigger to switch: roughly 150–300 orders/month or when per-order shipping+duty exceeds what 3PL economics would cost. Risk: capital locked in US inventory; wrong size/style guesses are expensive.
  - *Developer note:* Most 3PLs have native Shopify/WooCommerce apps — integration is mostly configuration, not custom code. Plan for split-inventory logic later (some SKUs in US, some India-only).
- ⚪ **Option C: Print-on-demand or US manufacturing — only for specific product types** — Printful/Printify print your designs on US-made blanks and ship domestically per order: zero customs, zero inventory, 2–7 day delivery. But unit costs are high ($12–18 for a printed tee before shipping), quality/fabric control is limited, and it only works for PRINTED basics — not for your own cut-and-sew designs, embroidery-heavy pieces, or India-made fabrics, which is usually the whole point of an India-based clothing brand. Full US manufacturing is far more expensive per unit than India. Verdict: useful as a side-lane for merch/basics, not as the core model.
  - *Developer note:* If ever used, POD apps integrate natively with all major platforms; treat POD SKUs as a separate fulfillment location.
- 🟢 **Realistic delivery-time table to plan around** — Set expectations with these door-to-door realities (including customs time): India Post 10–25 business days; economy express (Aramex/aggregator economy) 8–14 days; DHL/FedEx express 4–8 days; US 3PL domestic 2–5 days; POD 4–9 days (print time + ship). Customs can add 1–5 unpredictable days to any India-origin shipment, more if paperwork is wrong. Never promise the best case — promise the honest range and beat it.

### Duties, DDP vs DDU — who pays, and why you must pay

- 🟢 **Ship DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) — you pay duties, never your customer** — DDU/DAP means the courier knocks on your customer's door demanding $15–40 in duties + a brokerage fee before handing over a $40 hoodie. US shoppers are not used to this (until 2025 they never saw it), and it produces refused parcels, one-star reviews, and chargebacks. DDP means duty is calculated and paid by you upfront (built into your pricing); the customer pays exactly what checkout said. Every serious India-to-US D2C lane (Shiprocket X, ShipGlobal, DHL, even India Post's resumed US service) now supports DDP. Bake the duty into product prices or a shipping fee — do not surprise the customer.
  - *Developer note:* Checkout must display 'All duties & taxes included — no charges on delivery' near the shipping line. If the platform supports landed-cost calculation (Shopify Managed Markets, Zonos), duties can be itemized; otherwise fold into price.
- 🟢 **Never under-declare parcel values** — Marking a $60 hoodie as a '$10 gift' is customs fraud. Post-de-minimis, US Customs is actively screening low-value e-commerce parcels; consequences are seizures, fines, and your name on a naughty list that slows every future shipment. Declared value should match the actual sale price. Also declare honestly on the India side — export declarations feed your GST refunds.
- 🟡 **Understand fees beyond duty** — Per-order express shipments include the carrier's brokerage/disbursement fee (usually baked into DDP pricing, $5–20 per shipment). Bulk 3PL imports pay the Merchandise Processing Fee (~0.35% of value, minimum ~$33 per formal entry) and possibly harbor fees for sea freight. Small line items, but they belong in your landed-cost spreadsheet.

### Customs paperwork (both directions)

- 🟢 **Commercial invoice on every shipment** — Each parcel needs a commercial invoice: seller/buyer details, accurate item description ('men's 100% cotton knit t-shirt', not 'clothes'), quantity, unit value, total value, currency, HS code, country of origin, and terms (DDP). Aggregator platforms generate this automatically from order data — but only if your product data is complete and accurate.
  - *Developer note:* Ensure order line items carry description, HS code, unit price, and fiber content through to the carrier API payload.
- 🟢 **HS codes for every garment type** — Every product needs a Harmonized System code (garments live in HS chapters 61 knit / 62 woven — e.g., 6109.10 cotton t-shirts, 6110.20 cotton sweatshirts/hoodies, 6203/6204 trousers & dresses). The code determines the duty rate, and fiber content changes the code (cotton vs polyester versions of the same garment carry different rates). Get codes assigned once per product type — your courier/aggregator or a customs broker will do this in an hour — and store them on each product.
  - *Developer note:* Add an HS-code field (and fiber-composition field) per SKU in the product data model; required before first shipment.
- 🟢 **India export-side compliance: IEC, AD Code, GST LUT, CSB-V** — To export commercially from India you need: an IEC (Import Export Code, one-time, ~15 min online via DGFT); an AD Code registered with your bank at the port/courier terminal; and a GST LUT (Letter of Undertaking) so exports are zero-rated — you don't charge GST on export sales and can claim input GST refunds. Courier exports move on CSB-V shipping bills (the commercial courier category), which also makes you eligible for RoDTEP export incentives (~1–4% back). Aggregators handle CSB-V filing if your IEC/AD Code are registered with them. Without these, you're limited to 'gift'/sample channels — not a real business.

### Returns — the hardest part of international D2C

- 🟢 **Do NOT ask US customers to post returns back to India** — Return shipping US-to-India costs $25–60, takes weeks, and re-importing into India triggers its own customs mess (re-import of returned goods is duty-exemptible but paperwork-heavy). No US shopper will pay it, and if you pay it you lose money twice. Apparel return rates run 15–30% (sizing), so you MUST have a real plan, not a hope.
- 🟢 **Launch policy: returnless refunds + store credit + aggressive size guidance** — For a young brand, the cheapest 'returns infrastructure' is not needing the item back: refund or replace without return for items under a threshold (say $50), offer store credit with a bonus (e.g., 110% credit) as the first option, and cut return volume at the source with detailed size charts, model measurements, and fabric photos. US law has no federal 'must accept returns' rule, but your policy must be clearly posted (some states, e.g. California, require disclosure if you don't offer full refunds), and honored exactly as written.
  - *Developer note:* Refund flow should support refund-without-return and store-credit issuance. Size-guide content blocks per product template.
- 🟡 **Phase 2: a US return address / return hub** — Once volume grows, use a US returns hub: your logistics partner (ShipGlobal and Shiprocket X offer US returns solutions) or a third-party hub receives returns to a US address, inspects, and either restocks to your US 3PL, holds for consolidation, or liquidates. Customers get a normal domestic return label; you decide item-by-item whether it's worth anything. If you move to a US 3PL (Option B), returns handling comes almost free with it — another reason 3PL is the natural phase-2 move.
  - *Developer note:* A returns portal app (Loop, ReturnGO, AfterShip Returns) generates US labels and enforces policy rules automatically.
- ⚪ **Exchanges beat refunds** — Most apparel returns are size swaps. An exchange-first flow (send the new size, sometimes before the old one is back) keeps the revenue and the customer. Expensive to do from India per-order; natural once US stock exists.

### US labeling & packaging law for apparel (FTC Textile Act & friends)

- 🟢 **Fiber content label — mandatory** — The FTC Textile Fiber Products Identification Act requires every garment to carry a permanent label stating fiber content by percentage, in English, using generic fiber names ('100% Cotton', '60% Cotton / 40% Polyester' — not 'lycra', say 'spandex'). Non-compliant goods can be blocked at customs or fined. This must go into your PRODUCTION run — labels are sewn in, not stuck on later.
- 🟢 **Country of origin: 'Made in India' — permanent, and in the right place** — US Customs requires permanent, conspicuous origin marking. For garments with a neck, 'Made in India' must appear on the inside center of the neck (on or near the brand label). No neck (e.g., trousers) — a conspicuous inner label. Also required on the outer packaging/polybag if the label isn't visible through it. Removable stickers don't count.
- 🟢 **Business identity on the label: your company name (RN numbers are US-only)** — Labels must identify the manufacturer/importer/dealer. US companies use an FTC 'RN' number; foreign companies CANNOT get an RN — so use your full legal business name on the label instead (or, later, your US 3PL/importer's RN by arrangement). Decide the exact legal name string now so labels are printed correctly.
- 🟢 **Care labels — mandatory (FTC Care Labeling Rule)** — Permanent care instructions (wash/dry/iron/bleach guidance, words or approved symbols) must be attached and legible for the garment's life. You already likely have these for India; verify wording meets FTC norms in English.
- ⚪ **Flammability and children's wear — extra rules if applicable** — General adult apparel in standard fabrics normally passes CPSC flammability requirements by default, but sheer/raised-fiber fabrics can fail. Children's clothing is a different world: CPSIA testing + permanent tracking labels + children's sleepwear flammability standards. If you ever add a kids' line, budget for lab testing before shipping a single unit.
- 🟢 **Packaging for the customs journey and the brand moment** — Use waterproof polybag + sturdy mailer (parcels cross two countries and several sort centers); keep the commercial invoice in a packing-list pouch outside; skip anything that looks like undeclared extras (freebies also need declaring). Branded but lightweight — every 100 g is real money at international rates.

### Lost parcels, tracking, and US shopper expectations

- 🟢 **Tracked shipping on 100% of orders — non-negotiable** — US shoppers expect a tracking number within 24–48 h of ordering and a live view until delivery. Untracked shipping produces 'where is my order' tickets and 'item not received' chargebacks that you will lose without delivery proof. This rules out the cheapest untracked postal tiers entirely.
  - *Developer note:* Tracking number must sync from carrier API to the order record and fire the 'shipped' email automatically. Proof-of-delivery data is your chargeback evidence for Stripe/PayPal disputes.
- 🟢 **Set honest delivery expectations everywhere, and shoppers will accept 5–10 days** — US customers tolerate 5–10 business days for international delivery IF told upfront; they punish silence and surprises, not distance. Show 'Ships from our studio in India — arrives in 6–10 business days' on the product page, cart, and checkout, plus an FAQ. Amazon-speed is not expected from an indie brand; honesty is.
  - *Developer note:* Delivery-estimate text block near the add-to-cart button and in checkout shipping options; keep as editable config, not hard-coded.
- 🟢 **Legal note: the FTC Mail Order Rule on shipping delays** — US law (FTC's Mail/Internet Order Rule) says you must ship within the time you stated (or 30 days if unstated); if delayed, you must notify the customer and offer to cancel with a full refund. This is a real rule with real fines — build the habit of a 24–48 h dispatch SLA and delay notifications.
  - *Developer note:* A simple report/alert for orders unfulfilled after X days covers the compliance workflow.
- 🟢 **Lost/stuck parcel playbook** — Some parcels will vanish or stall in customs. Decide the policy NOW: if tracking is dead for 10+ days, proactively reship or refund without making the customer fight (this converts a disaster into loyalty). File the carrier claim in parallel — express carriers cover ~$100 by default; buy shipment insurance (1–2% of value) or self-insure by building ~1–2% loss into pricing. Track your loss rate per carrier and drop bad lanes.
- 🟡 **Notifications: shipped → in transit → customs → out for delivery → delivered** — Branded tracking page + automatic email at each milestone kills most support tickets before they're born. Email at launch; SMS is a phase-2 nicety (US carriers charge per SMS; email is free).
  - *Developer note:* AfterShip or the aggregator's white-label tracking page is a low-code win; embed under the store's domain.
- 🟡 **Signature on delivery for high-value orders** — Porch theft is a real US phenomenon. Above ~$150 order value, require signature or route via carrier pickup points to cut 'delivered but not received' disputes.

### US privacy — CCPA/CPRA and state privacy laws

- 🟢 **US-audience privacy policy (rewrite, not translate)** — California's CalOPPA requires a privacy policy from ANY site collecting personal info from California residents — no size threshold. The policy must say what you collect, why, who you share it with (Shopify, Meta, Google, Klaviyo, courier), whether you 'sell or share' data for advertising, and how to make a request. Serving it from India does not exempt you — you are selling to US consumers.
  - *Developer note:* CMS-editable policy page linked in the footer sitewide. Include: categories of data collected, purposes, third parties, retention, CCPA/CPRA rights (know/delete/correct/opt-out), a contact method for requests, and an effective date. Use a US-lawyer-reviewed template (Termly/Enzuzo generators are an acceptable start; do not reuse the DPDP policy).
- 🟢 **'Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information' link + honoring Global Privacy Control** — Under CPRA, letting Meta/TikTok/Google pixels track your visitors counts as 'sharing' personal info for advertising. Compliant US stores show an opt-out link in the footer and honor the browser's Global Privacy Control signal. The big CCPA thresholds ($25M+ revenue or 100k+ consumers) mean full enforcement likely won't hit you at first, but pixels make this the one part worth doing correctly from day one — it is cheap now and expensive to retrofit.
  - *Developer note:* Footer link 'Your Privacy Choices' opening an opt-out control. Wire pixels through GTM/Consent Mode (or Shopify's Customer Privacy API) so an opt-out or a GPC signal actually stops Meta/TikTok/GA ad cookies. Log opt-outs. Tools: Shopify's built-in privacy banner, Termly, Osano, or Enzuzo — pick one that supports GPC.
- 🟢 **US-style cookie notice (not an EU-style blocking banner)** — US practice is a light notice with an opt-out, not a wall that blocks the site. An aggressive EU-style banner costs conversions US shoppers don't expect. If you never target the EU/UK, you don't need consent-before-cookies for US visitors in most states.
  - *Developer note:* Non-blocking banner or footer link is sufficient for US-only targeting; geo-target the stricter behavior only if EU traffic is later served. Don't let the CMP block page render (keeps Core Web Vitals intact).
- 🟢 **Privacy request process: access, deletion, correction** — California and a dozen-plus other states (Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas, Oregon and more) give consumers rights to see, delete, and fix their data. At your size a documented email-based process is fine — but it must actually work within 45 days.
  - *Developer note:* privacy@ email + a simple internal runbook: verify identity, export/delete from Shopify + Klaviyo + review app + support tool, respond within 45 days. Self-serve portal is a later item. Retain order/export records required for Indian tax law and note that carve-out in the policy.
- 🟢 **Notice at collection points** — California expects a short notice at or before the point where you collect data (signup forms, checkout) linking to the privacy policy — same idea as the DPDP notice you already scoped, different wording.
  - *Developer note:* One-line 'By signing up you agree to our Privacy Policy' with link at newsletter popup, footer signup, account creation, and checkout marketing checkbox. Marketing opt-in checkbox stays un-ticked.
- ⚪ **COPPA — no marketing or tracking aimed at children under 13** — If the site or ads knowingly collect data from under-13s, federal COPPA fines apply. As an adult apparel brand you just need to not target kids and not offer a kids' line without revisiting this.
  - *Developer note:* No age-gated content needed; just avoid child-directed creative and audiences. Revisit if kidswear launches.
- 🟡 **Data breach notification plan (all 50 states have breach laws)** — Every US state requires notifying affected residents after a personal-data breach, each with slightly different rules. A one-page plan (who investigates, who drafts the notice, when a lawyer is called) is enough at this stage.
  - *Developer note:* Mostly mitigated by keeping card data with the payment processor and minimizing stored data. Document which systems hold US customer PII (Shopify, Klaviyo, support tool) so a breach scope can be assessed fast.

### Email marketing law — CAN-SPAM (plus Gmail/Yahoo deliverability rules)

- 🟢 **Physical postal address in every marketing email** — CAN-SPAM requires a valid physical postal address in every commercial email, with penalties per email in the tens of thousands of dollars. Your Indian business address is legally acceptable, but a US virtual mailbox address looks more trustworthy to US shoppers and doubles as a returns-correspondence address.
  - *Developer note:* Set the address in Klaviyo account settings so it auto-appends to every template footer. Options: real India address, or a US virtual address service (e.g., a commercial mail-receiving agency); either satisfies the law.
- 🟢 **Working unsubscribe honored within 10 business days** — Every marketing email needs a clear unsubscribe that works without a login and takes effect within 10 business days. Klaviyo does this automatically — the risk is only if anyone ever sends manual blasts outside the tool.
  - *Developer note:* Only send marketing through the ESP; never BCC blasts from Gmail. Suppress unsubscribes across all lists, not per-list.
- 🟢 **Truthful 'From' names and subject lines** — CAN-SPAM bans misleading sender names and deceptive subject lines ('RE: your order' when there is no order). This is also an FTC deception issue.
- 🟢 **One-click unsubscribe headers + email authentication (Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules)** — Not a law but effectively mandatory: Gmail and Yahoo block bulk senders without SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click list-unsubscribe, and a spam-complaint rate under 0.3%. Already in the India scope — it carries over unchanged and matters even more since US inboxes are Gmail-heavy.
  - *Developer note:* Send marketing from a subdomain (mail.brand.com); DMARC at least p=none with reporting; warm up the sending domain before BFCM volume.
- 🟢 **Understand: US email is opt-out based — but collect opt-ins anyway** — Unlike India/EU, CAN-SPAM technically lets you email people who never opted in. Don't. Purchased lists and cold blasts destroy deliverability and trust; the practical US norm is confirmed opt-in via popup and checkout checkbox.

### SMS marketing law — TCPA and 10DLC (treat texting as high-risk, phase 2)

- 🟡 **TCPA express written consent before any marketing text** — The TCPA is the most-litigated consumer law in America — $500 to $1,500 per text in statutory damages, and class-action firms actively hunt small e-commerce brands. You need clear written consent (checkbox with specific disclosure language) BEFORE the first marketing SMS. Order-status texts to a number given at checkout are lower risk, but promotional texts without documented consent can bankrupt a small brand.
  - *Developer note:* Use a compliant capture flow only (Klaviyo SMS/Postscript/Attentive built-in forms with their lawyer-approved disclosure: 'By signing up you agree to receive recurring automated marketing texts... Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to cancel'). Store consent timestamp, source, and the exact disclosure shown. Never import phone numbers collected under the India-scope consent language.
- 🟡 **10DLC registration through the SMS platform** — US carriers require business texting to be registered (brand + campaign) or messages get filtered. Your SMS platform handles the paperwork — this is the US replacement for India's DLT, but faster and platform-managed.
  - *Developer note:* Registration needs a business identity — a US EIN makes approval smoother; foreign entities can register but expect more friction and lower throughput. Factor this into the US-entity decision.
- 🟡 **Quiet hours, STOP/HELP handling, and state mini-TCPA laws** — Texts must stay within roughly 8am–9pm in the RECIPIENT'S time zone (some states like Florida and Oklahoma are stricter), STOP must always work instantly, and revocation must be honored quickly. Platforms enforce most of this if configured.
  - *Developer note:* Enable platform quiet-hours by recipient timezone (not IST!), honor STOP across channels, cap frequency. Set send schedules in US time zones for campaigns too.
- 🟢 **Recommendation: launch email-only, add SMS in phase 2** — Email covers cart recovery and campaigns at launch with near-zero legal risk. SMS adds revenue but carries the highest lawsuit risk of anything in this addendum — add it once policies, consent flows, and the entity question are settled.

### FTC rules — reviews, influencers, pricing, returns, origin claims

- 🟢 **No fake, seeded, or filtered reviews (FTC Consumer Review Rule)** — Since late 2024 the FTC can fine roughly $50k+ PER fake review — including reviews by you/family/employees without disclosure, bought reviews, and 'review gating' (only asking happy customers while suppressing unhappy ones). Your India scope already bans fake reviews; the US penalty regime has real teeth.
  - *Developer note:* Verified-buyer-only reviews tied to delivered orders; publish negatives and positives alike (moderate only profanity/off-topic); if a review was incentivized (free product, discount), it must be labeled. Review apps: Judge.me, Okendo, Loox — configure to not gate.
- 🟡 **Influencer disclosure — #ad and material-connection rules** — FTC Endorsement Guides require anyone you pay OR gift product to clearly disclose the relationship (#ad, 'gifted') in a way viewers can't miss. The brand is liable too, not just the creator. This is standard practice in US influencer marketing — contracts must require it.
  - *Developer note:* Add an FTC-disclosure clause and usage-rights clause to every creator agreement, even for gifted seeding. Keep records. Applies to your own employees posting about the brand as well.
- 🟢 **Truthful strikethrough / 'compare at' pricing** — Fake former prices ('was $120, now $49' when it never sold at $120) drive class actions under California and other state laws, and FTC deception rules. Only strike through a price the product genuinely sold at for a reasonable period.
  - *Developer note:* Keep a price-history log per product. No perpetual 'sales'. No fabricated countdown timers or fake low-stock badges (carries over from the India dark-patterns item — the US legal exposure is state consumer-protection suits).
- 🟢 **Clear return/refund policy + honor it (and state-specific display rules)** — The FTC requires refund/return terms to be clearly disclosed before purchase, and some states (notably California and New York) require the policy to be conspicuously posted — in California, if you don't post a policy, customers get a default 30-day full-refund right. Fashion is return-heavy; the policy page is a legal document, not just a trust page.
  - *Developer note:* Returns policy linked in footer, on product pages near the buy button, and at checkout. State window (30 days is the US norm), condition requirements, who pays return shipping, refund method and timeline.
- 🟢 **FTC Mail/Internet Order Rule — ship when you say you will** — Federal rule: you must ship within the time you advertise (or 30 days if none stated); if delayed, you must notify the customer and offer a full refund. With production in India, honest realistic ship-time promises on the site are a legal requirement, not just good UX.
  - *Developer note:* Show a realistic 'ships within X business days' per product (config-driven, changeable during BFCM); build an automated delay-notification email with a cancel-for-refund link for orders that miss the promised window.
- 🟢 **'Made in India' origin — required on labels, never imply US origin** — US customs and the FTC Textile Act require country of origin ('Made in India') on every garment's label and it must not be contradicted by marketing. Never say or imply 'Made in USA'. Good news: 'Made in India' craftsmanship can be a positive brand story for US shoppers — lean into it rather than hiding it.
  - *Developer note:* Origin on the sewn-in label and accurate on the product page. 'Designed in [city]' claims must be literally true. Do not geo-hide origin info.
- 🟢 **FTC textile labeling — fiber content, RN or legal name, care instructions** — Every garment sold in the US must carry a label with fiber content percentages ('100% Cotton'), country of origin, the manufacturer/importer identity (a registered RN number or full legal company name), and care instructions (FTC Care Labeling Rule). This replaces India's Legal Metrology tag requirements and must go to your tag printer BEFORE production.
  - *Developer note:* Also mirror fiber content and care info on the product page (US shoppers check). An RN number is free from the FTC but requires a US business presence — a foreign company can use its full legal name on labels instead.
- ⚪ **Auto-renewal / negative-option rules (only if subscriptions ever launch)** — If you ever add a subscription box or membership, federal and California rules require clear terms, express consent, and easy one-click cancellation. Not relevant for a standard store.

### ADA website accessibility

- 🟢 **Build to WCAG 2.1 AA from day one** — US serial plaintiffs file thousands of ADA lawsuits a year against e-commerce sites — small fashion brands are common targets, and settling typically costs $5k–$25k plus fixes. Courts and the DOJ treat WCAG 2.1 AA as the de facto standard. Building it in now is far cheaper than being sued into it.
  - *Developer note:* Concretely: alt text on all product images, full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, 4.5:1 text contrast, labeled form fields, ARIA on the mini-cart/menus/modals, captions on videos, no information conveyed by color alone. Test with axe DevTools + a screen reader pass (VoiceOver) on home → product → checkout. Avoid 'accessibility overlay' widgets (accessiBe etc.) — they don't prevent lawsuits and are themselves criticized.
- 🟡 **Accessibility statement page** — A page stating your commitment, the standard you target, and a contact for accessibility problems deters drive-by lawsuits and helps real users.
  - *Developer note:* Footer-linked page; include an email for accessibility feedback and a promise to respond.
- ⚪ **Periodic accessibility audit** — A professional audit once the site is stable catches what automated tools miss (roughly 70% of issues are manual-only).

### US trademark & brand protection

- 🟢 **USPTO clearance search BEFORE locking the brand name** — Vawehall is still a placeholder — before committing to any name, search the USPTO database (and common-law use: Google, Instagram, Amazon) for conflicts in clothing. Discovering a US conflict after printing labels and building the domain is the expensive version. An Indian trademark gives you zero rights in the US.
- 🟢 **File US trademark application — foreign applicants MUST use a US-licensed attorney** — Since 2019 the USPTO requires foreign-domiciled applicants to file through a US-licensed attorney — you cannot self-file from India. File in Class 25 (clothing) and Class 35 (online retail). Expect roughly $250–350 per class in government fees plus $500–1,500 attorney fees; registration takes 12–18 months but your priority date is set at filing.
  - *Developer note:* You can file 'intent to use' before the store launches. Alternative route: extend an Indian application to the US via the Madrid Protocol — discuss both with the attorney.
- 🟡 **Ignore trademark scam mail** — After filing, you WILL receive official-looking invoices from fake 'trademark registries' demanding fees. All real correspondence comes through your attorney. Pay nothing that doesn't come from them.
- 🟢 **Secure .com and US social handles for the final name** — For US customers the .com is the trust default (.in reads as foreign). Grab the .com, Instagram, TikTok, and Pinterest handles the moment the name is finalized.

### Terms of service, jurisdiction & the US-entity question

- 🟢 **US-facing Terms of Service with a deliberate governing-law choice** — Your ToS must choose whose law governs and where disputes are heard. 'Courts of New Delhi, Indian law' is technically possible but scares US shoppers, may be ignored by US courts for consumer claims, and looks evasive. The common approach for foreign sellers: a US state's law (often Delaware or New York) with an arbitration clause. This is a real lawyer decision, not a template blank.
  - *Developer note:* ToS page CMS-editable, footer-linked, with checkout acknowledgment ('By placing your order you agree to our Terms') — courts enforce clickwrap far better than a buried link. Include order-acceptance terms, pricing-error rights, and IP ownership of site content.
- 🟢 **Arbitration clause + class-action waiver** — Standard armor for US e-commerce: disputes go to individual arbitration instead of class actions. US courts generally enforce these when properly presented. For a small foreign seller this is the single biggest litigation-risk reducer available in a document.
  - *Developer note:* Must be drafted by the US lawyer (state-specific carve-outs, small-claims exception, opt-out window are needed for enforceability).
- 🟢 **Limitation of liability & warranty disclaimers** — US law implies warranties on goods sold; the ToS should disclaim what's legally disclaimable and cap your liability at the order value. Boilerplate for a US lawyer, dangerous to improvise.
- 🟢 **Decide: US LLC or sell as an Indian entity** — Many Indian D2C founders form a US LLC (Delaware/Wyoming, via services like Stripe Atlas or doola, roughly $500–800 to set up). Benefits: cleaner payment processing (full Shopify Payments/Stripe access), a US bank account, easier 10DLC SMS registration, US-address trust signals, and a liability shield between US lawsuits and your Indian assets. Costs: annual filings and US tax returns (~$1–2k/yr in compliance). You CAN launch as an Indian entity (Shopify + PayPal/2Checkout/Payoneer routes exist), but decide this early because payments, ToS, insurance, and SMS all hang off it.
  - *Developer note:* This decision blocks payment-gateway setup — sequence it first. If a US LLC is formed, the LLC becomes the contracting party in ToS/Privacy Policy and the named insured on liability insurance. Coordinate with the payments/tax addendum owner.

### Product liability & safety basics

- 🟢 **Flammability compliance for apparel (CPSC / Flammable Fabrics Act)** — All clothing sold in the US must meet federal flammability standards (16 CFR 1610). Most normal-weight cottons and blends pass easily or are exempt, but sheer/lightweight fabrics, brushed/raised-surface fabrics, and rayon can fail. Your Indian manufacturer should confirm fabric compliance; a testing lab report per risky fabric costs little compared to a CPSC recall.
  - *Developer note:* Keep supplier fabric specs and any test reports on file per style. Plain-surface fabrics over ~2.6 oz/sq yd are exempt from testing — most streetwear/casualwear qualifies, but verify anything sheer, fuzzy, or flowing.
- ⚪ **Do NOT launch children's clothing without a separate compliance workstream** — Kids' products trigger CPSIA: mandatory third-party lab testing, lead/phthalate limits, tracking labels, Children's Product Certificates, and strict drawstring/sleepwear rules. It roughly doubles compliance cost per style. Adults-only at launch keeps this whole area closed.
- 🟢 **Product liability insurance covering US & Canada jurisdiction** — The US is the world's most litigious market and one injury claim (allergic reaction to dye, a burn, a choking drawstring) can exceed everything you own. Standard Indian liability policies EXCLUDE US/Canada jurisdiction — you need a policy that explicitly covers US sales, either a US policy (if you form an LLC) or an Indian exporter's policy with a US/Canada extension. Budget roughly $500–$1,500/year at your volume.
- ⚪ **Basic incident/recall readiness** — If a product turns out unsafe, US law requires reporting to the CPSC. At your stage this just means: keep batch/style records so you could identify affected orders, and know that cpsc.gov is where a report goes.
  - *Developer note:* Order records already link customer to SKU/batch if the admin stores a production-lot field per style — a cheap field to add.

### US sizing & size-chart expectations

- 🟢 **US size conventions on every product** — US women's wear uses numeric sizes (0–16+) and letter sizes with US grading — a US 'M' is larger than an Indian 'M'. Men's uses inch-based waist/chest. Selling Indian-graded S/M/L unconverted is the #1 cause of returns and angry reviews for India-origin brands. Sizes must be converted at the production/labeling stage, not just on the website.
  - *Developer note:* Product variants named in US conventions; garment labels printed with US sizes. Keep an internal India↔US mapping doc for the manufacturer.
- 🟢 **Size chart in INCHES with body + garment measurements** — US shoppers expect inches (never cm-only), both body measurements ('fits bust 36–38"') and garment measurements ('chest width 21"'), per garment type. A good chart is the single best returns-reducer for a brand nobody has tried on.
  - *Developer note:* Reusable size-chart component per product type, inches primary with a cm toggle, embedded on the product page (modal or inline), CMS-editable. Carries over the India scope's size-guide item with units flipped.
- 🟢 **Fit notes and model stats on product pages** — US product-page convention: 'Model is 5'9" wearing size S' plus a fit descriptor ('runs small — size up', 'relaxed fit'). Shoppers rely on these more than the chart.
  - *Developer note:* Structured fields: model height (feet/inches), model size worn, fit scale (runs small/true/runs large). Later: 'true to size' voting in the review app.
- 🟡 **Extended/inclusive sizing plan** — The average US woman wears roughly a size 16–18; US shoppers increasingly expect ranges through XL–3X. Not mandatory at launch, but a known growth lever and a common complaint if absent.

### Shipping-speed messaging & US trust signals

- 🟢 **Honest delivery-window messaging everywhere** — US shoppers are Amazon-trained to expect 2-day shipping; you will likely deliver in 8–15 days from India (or 2–5 days if you later stock a US 3PL). The gap is survivable ONLY if the site is upfront: estimated delivery dates on the product page, cart, and checkout. Hidden slow shipping = chargebacks, 'where is my order' tickets, and 1-star reviews. This also ties into the FTC ship-time rule above.
  - *Developer note:* Show a date range ('Arrives Aug 28 – Sep 4'), not just 'ships in 5 days'. Config-driven transit assumptions per method. Order-status page + email/SMS tracking updates carry over from the India scope.
- 🟢 **Position the origin story instead of hiding it** — 'Ships from our studio in India' framed as craftsmanship ('cut and sewn in small batches in Jaipur') converts better than an anonymous slow delivery that shoppers assume is dropshipping. US shoppers forgive transit time for a story; they punish it when it feels like deception.
- 🟢 **No surprise customs charges — sell landed-cost (DDP)** — US de minimis duty-free treatment for low-value parcels ended in 2025, so India-origin parcels can attract duties. If a customer gets a surprise bill from the carrier at their door, you lose them forever and get a chargeback. Price and ship duties-paid so the checkout total is final. (Mechanics belong to the shipping/tax addendum — the marketing requirement is: the site must be able to promise 'no customs fees, ever'.)
  - *Developer note:* DDP via the chosen cross-border carrier; a 'Taxes & duties included — no surprise fees' line at checkout and in the FAQ.
- 🟢 **US returns that don't require shipping back to India** — US shoppers check the returns policy before first purchase from an unknown brand; 'ship your return to Mumbai at your cost' kills conversion. Standard solutions: a US returns address via a 3PL or returns aggregator, or 'returnless refunds' on low-value items. Free returns are the US ideal; a flat-fee return label is acceptable if stated clearly.
  - *Developer note:* Returns portal (Loop, AfterShip Returns, or Shopify native) issuing US domestic labels to a US consolidation address; policy page states window, condition, and fee clearly. Decision needed from owner + shipping-area plan.
- 🟢 **Trust-signal package US shoppers scan for** — Before buying from an unknown site, US shoppers look for: real customer reviews with photos, a clear returns policy, HTTPS padlock, a physical address and working contact page, recognizable payment marks (Visa/Mastercard/Amex/PayPal/Shop Pay/Apple Pay), and a coherent About page. Absence of any one of these reads as 'scam site' — especially for a foreign seller.
  - *Developer note:* Trust strip near the buy button ('Free returns · Secure checkout · Taxes & duties included'); footer with contact address; payment icons rendered by the checkout provider; About page with founder story and real photos. The India scope's trust-badge item carries over with US-relevant content (drop 'COD available', 'UPI').
- 🟢 **US-timezone customer support expectations** — US customers expect replies within one business day, in fluent English, during roughly US business hours. India time zones actually help (your morning covers US evening) — but published support hours and a real email must exist. Phone support is optional at launch; a contact form alone looks evasive.
  - *Developer note:* Support email + shared inbox/helpdesk; auto-reply stating response time; contact page shows email, address, and hours in ET/PT.
